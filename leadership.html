Leadership and service

I have been at CSU Bakersfield since 2023. Most of what follows is infrastructure rather than scholarship: a Hub with a room and machines in it, a colloquium series that brings outside researchers to campus, coordination of MATH 1209 and MATH 2200, a proposed degree, a faculty research group, and the research computing the rest of it depends on.

The common thread is straightforward — students and colleagues at CSUB should be able to do serious statistical and AI work here, with the tools and the people that requires.

Founded, organized and coordinated at CSUB

  • 2025 – present

    CSUB Hub for Statistics, Applied AI & Data Science Innovation

    Founder and Faculty Lead

    I founded the Hub and lead it. It is an interdisciplinary home for statistics, applied AI, data science, student research, research computing, and cross-disciplinary collaboration at CSUB. Two things had to exist first: a physical space, which the Hub now has, and computing infrastructure that undergraduate and faculty research can actually run on, which I developed.

  • 2026 – present

    Statistics Coordinator, Department of Mathematics

Curriculum

Degree programs and new courses

With Statistics and Mathematics faculty

  • Degree

    Statistical Data Science concentration, and a proposed B.S.

    2025 – present

    I work with Statistics and Mathematics faculty on CSUB’s Statistical Data Science curriculum: the existing concentration, and the current effort to develop a standalone B.S. in Statistical Data Science. My contributions cover degree requirements, prerequisite structure, elective pathways, comparisons with programs at other universities, student-demand survey analysis, and proposal development.

  • MATH 4230

    Applied Statistical Methods for Data Science

    2025 – 2026 · Taught Spring 2026

    Co-developed with Statistics faculty as an upper-division applied statistics and data science course; I taught it in Spring 2026. I built that implementation — syllabus, instructional materials, computational labs, assessments, and an open-ended capstone centered on real data, reproducible workflows, and statistical communication — using R, Python, JupyterHub, and structured approaches to responsible AI use. I took part in the 2026 National Workshop on Data Science Education at UC Berkeley to benchmark the course and the wider curriculum.

  • MATH 3218

    The Science of Data, Statistics, and Artificial Intelligence

    2025 · New upper-division GE course

    I participated in the faculty review and refinement of this new upper-division GE course, led by Dr. Eduardo Montoya, and served as the named proposer responsible for entering and advancing the proposal through CourseLeaf.

  • Proposal

    Inside AI: The Mathematics of Large Language Models

    Lower-division Area 2 · Initial proposal

    A proposed lower-division Area 2 course (Mathematical Concepts & Quantitative Reasoning) that teaches mathematics and statistics through the inner workings of modern language models: tokens, embeddings, vectors and matrices, attention, probability, sampling, uncertainty, numerical precision, quantization, model size, and statistical evaluation. The concept adapts hands-on small-model teaching ideas I encountered through Eric Van Dusen’s UC Berkeley and NWDSE materials to a mathematics-and-statistics GE setting. I developed the initial proposal and course material.

Infrastructure

Research computing and AI infrastructure

2025 – present

I contribute to the research-computing infrastructure supporting statistics, data science, and AI research at CSUB, including the connection to the National Research Platform / Nautilus ecosystem for GPU and high-performance computing.

I also take part in CSUB’s AWS Cloud & AI initiative as a faculty collaborator and project team member, contributing statistical evaluation and benchmarking, curriculum-integration ideas, and student-training plans. The collaborative CSUB project was awarded $200,000 in AWS cloud credits in 2026.

Training

AI and data science workshops

2025 – present

I have developed and delivered a sustained program of workshops, seminars, and training activities on responsible and useful applications of generative AI in teaching and research. Selected sessions include Teaching With AI: Helping Students Learn Statistics With Custom GPTs and Prompts, AI as a Thinking Partner in Statistics, and Effective Ways to Introduce AI in Your Classroom at CSUB.

I have also developed course-specific AI tutoring approaches to support mathematical and statistical reasoning, and contributed to broader campus AI activities, including OpenAI University Day at CSUB.
