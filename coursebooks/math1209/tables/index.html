Statistical Tables

This appendix collects the two tables MATH 1209 needs all semester: the standard Normal (z) table (Ch 4–7) and the Student t table (Ch 7). Both answer the same kind of question two different ways — “how much area?” or “what cutoff value?” — and both have a calculator shortcut and an R shortcut, so you never have to read the printed grid unless you want to check your work by hand. Every value below was computed directly (not copied from another book), so it matches exactly what invNorm, normalcdf, qnorm, and qt return on your own calculator or in R.

11. The standard Normal (z) table¶

The z-table gives the area to the left of a z-score under the standard Normal curve N(0,1)N(0,1)N(0,1) — that is, P(Z<z)P(Z < z)P(Z<z). Find the whole-number-and-tenths part of your z-score down the left column, then the hundredths part across the top row; the cell where they meet is the area.

How to read it

Look up z=1.42z = 1.42z=1.42: find the row 1.4, then the column .02. The cell reads 0.9222, meaning about 92.22% of the standard Normal curve lies below z=1.42z = 1.42z=1.42. To find the area above a value, subtract from 1: P(Z>1.42)=1−0.9222=0.0778P(Z > 1.42) = 1 - 0.9222 = 0.0778P(Z>1.42)=1−0.9222=0.0778. To find the area between two z-scores, subtract the smaller table value from the larger one.

22. The Student t table¶

The t-distribution is used instead of the Normal whenever you estimate a mean’s standard error from the sample standard deviation sss rather than knowing the true σ\sigmaσ — which is the everyday case in Ch 7. It is bell-shaped and centered at 0 like the Normal, but with heavier tails; its exact shape depends on the degrees of freedom, df=n−1df = n - 1df=n−1 for one-sample work. Unlike the z-table, the t-table does not print every possible area — it prints only the critical values (t∗t^{*}t∗) you need for common confidence levels and significance levels, because that is what a confidence interval or a hypothesis-test decision actually requires.

How to read it

Find your dfdfdf down the left column, then the column that matches your confidence level or significance level, and read off t∗t^{*}t∗. For a 95% confidence interval with n=24n = 24n=24 (so df=23df = 23df=23), use the “0.025 one-tail / 95% CI” column: t∗=2.069t^{*} = 2.069t∗=2.069. For a one-sided test at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05 with df=23df = 23df=23, use the “0.05 one-tail” column: t=1.714t = 1.714t=1.714 is your cutoff. If your exact dfdfdf is not listed (the table skips from 30 to 40), use the next smaller dfdfdf shown — it gives a slightly wider, more conservative interval — or get the exact value with invT or qt().

Notice the bottom row: as dfdfdf grows, every column in the t-table slides down toward the matching z-critical value from the Course Formula Sheet (1.645 / 1.960 / 2.576 for 90% / 95% / 99%).
