Glossary

Every key term introduced across the fifteen weekly units, gathered here with one concise definition and organized alphabetically. Symbols are defined where they appear; the Course Formula Sheet collects the matching formulas.

1A¶

  • addition rule (disjoint) — For disjoint events, P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B).

  • addition rule (general) — For any two events, P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A and B)P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A and B); the last term removes double-counted outcomes.

  • alternative hypothesis (HAH_AHA​) — The claim you suspect might be true, stated with <<<, >>>, or ≠\neq=.

  • approximately normal — Close enough to bell-shaped and symmetric (mean near median, no long one-sided tail) that the Normal model gives trustworthy probabilities.

  • association — Two categorical variables are associated when the conditional proportions of one change noticeably across categories of the other.

2B¶

  • bar chart — A graph of categorical frequencies or relative frequencies as bar heights; the y-axis must start at 0 to be read honestly.

  • bias — A sampling method’s systematic tendency to miss the true population value; it does not shrink with a bigger sample.

3C¶

  • case (observation) — One row of a data table; one individual or unit.

  • categorical (qualitative) variable — A variable that sorts cases into groups: nominal (no natural order) or ordinal (a natural order).

  • Central Limit Theorem (CLT) — For a large enough sample, the sampling distribution of a sample proportion or mean is approximately Normal, centered at the true parameter, regardless of the population’s shape.

  • cluster sample — Randomly selected whole groups (clusters), with every member of a chosen cluster included.

  • complement (AcA^cAc) — The event “AAA does not happen”; P(Ac)=1−P(A)P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)P(Ac)=1−P(A).

  • conditional proportion — The proportion of one variable’s categories within a single row or column of the other.

  • confidence interval — A range of plausible values for a parameter, built as point estimate ± margin of error; for a mean, xˉ±t∗ s/n\bar{x} \pm t^{*}\, s/\sqrt{n}xˉ±t∗s/n​.

  • confidence level — The long-run success rate of the interval-building procedure (e.g., about 95% of 95% intervals capture the true parameter).

  • confounding variable — A variable tangled up with both the explanatory and response variables, clouding a causal read.

  • continuous random variable — A random variable that can take any value along a continuum, so its values cannot be listed in a table.

  • critical value (z∗z^{*}z∗ or t∗t^{*}t∗) — The number of standard errors reached on each side of a point estimate for a stated confidence level; z∗z^{*}z∗ = 1.645 / 1.960 / 2.576 for 90% / 95% / 99%, while t∗t^{*}t∗ is read from a t-table by degrees of freedom.

  • cutoff / threshold — The specific data value marking a probability boundary (e.g., “the score for the top 15%”).

4D¶

  • degrees of freedom (dfdfdf) — For one-mean inference, df=n−1df = n - 1df=n−1; it fixes the exact shape of the t-distribution.

  • density curve — A smooth curve for a continuous random variable where area under the curve equals probability; it is never negative and its total area is 1.

  • deviation — How far a value sits from the mean, x−μx - \mux−μ; can be positive or negative.

  • discrete probability distribution — A table of every possible value xxx of a discrete random variable with its probability P(x)P(x)P(x); always ∑P(x)=1\sum P(x) = 1∑P(x)=1.

  • disjoint (mutually exclusive) events — Events that cannot both happen on the same outcome, so P(A and B)=0P(A \text{ and } B) = 0P(A and B)=0.

5E¶

  • empirical rule (68–95–99.7 rule) — For a Normal model, about 68% of values fall within 1 SD of the mean, 95% within 2 SD, and 99.7% within 3 SD.

  • event — A subset of the sample space; something that either happens or not on a given trial.

  • expected value (E(X)E(X)E(X), μ\muμ) — ∑x⋅P(x)\sum x \cdot P(x)∑x⋅P(x); the probability-weighted, long-run average of a random variable.

  • experiment — A study in which researchers deliberately assign subjects to treatments.

  • explanatory variable / response variable — The possible cause, and the outcome it might explain.

6F¶

  • five-number summary — Minimum, Q1Q_1Q1​, median, Q3Q_3Q3​, maximum.

  • frequency — A raw count of observations in a category.

7I¶

  • independent events — Events for which knowing whether one happened gives no information about the other; then P(A and B)=P(A)P(B)P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)P(A and B)=P(A)P(B).

  • interquartile range (IQR) — Q3−Q1Q_3 - Q_1Q3​−Q1​; the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

  • invNorm(area, μ, σ) — TI-83/84 function returning the value with a given area to its left (the inverse-normal / percentile problem); matches xqnorm() in R.

8J¶

  • joint probability (P(A and B)P(A \text{ and } B)P(A and B)) — The probability that both AAA and BBB happen.

  • joint proportion — The proportion of all observations in one specific cell of a two-way table (both variables at once).

9L¶

  • Law of Large Numbers — As repetitions grow, the observed relative frequency of an event settles toward its true probability.

10M¶

  • margin of error (ME) — The distance an interval reaches on either side of the point estimate; ME=(critical value)×SEME = (\text{critical value}) \times SEME=(critical value)×SE.

  • marginal proportion — A proportion for one variable read from a table’s row or column total, ignoring the other variable.

  • mean (xˉ\bar{x}xˉ) — The arithmetic average, ∑xi/n\sum x_i / n∑xi​/n; pulled toward outliers and skew.

  • median — The middle value of sorted data; resistant to outliers and skew.

  • misleading graph — A graph (e.g., one with a truncated y-axis) that distorts the visual comparison without changing the underlying numbers.

  • mode — The most frequently occurring value.

  • multiplication rule (independent events) — P(A and B)=P(A)×P(B)P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)P(A and B)=P(A)×P(B), valid only when the events are independent.

11N¶

  • normal distribution (N(μ,σ)N(\mu, \sigma)N(μ,σ)) — A symmetric, unimodal, bell-shaped density curve centered at μ\muμ with spread controlled by σ\sigmaσ.

  • normalcdf(lower, upper, μ, σ) — TI-83/84 function returning the area (probability) between two cutoffs on a Normal curve; matches xpnorm() in R.

  • null hypothesis (H0H_0H0​) — The skeptical “nothing new” claim about a parameter, stated as an equality and assumed true unless the data give strong evidence otherwise.

  • null proportion (p0p_0p0​) / null mean (μ0\mu_0μ0​) — The specific value claimed under H0H_0H0​; p0p_0p0​ (not p^\hat{p}p^​) is used inside a proportion test’s standard error.

  • numerical (quantitative) variable — A variable whose values can be meaningfully averaged: discrete (counted) or continuous (measured on a scale).

12O¶

  • observational study — A study in which subjects are measured, not assigned, to groups.

  • one-proportion z-interval / z-test — Ch 6 procedures for estimating (p^±z∗SE\hat{p} \pm z^{*}SEp^​±z∗SE) or testing (z=(p^−p0)/p0(1−p0)/nz = (\hat{p} - p_0)/\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}z=(p^​−p0​)/p0​(1−p0​)/n​) a single population proportion.

  • one-sample t-test — Tests H0:μ=μ0H_0: \mu = \mu_0H0​:μ=μ0​ using t=(xˉ−μ0)/(s/n)t = (\bar{x} - \mu_0)/(s/\sqrt{n})t=(xˉ−μ0​)/(s/n​) with df=n−1df = n - 1df=n−1.

  • one-sided / two-sided test — A test whose HAH_AHA​ points in one direction (<<< or >>>) versus either direction (≠\neq=).

  • outlier — A value below Q1−1.5⋅IQRQ_1 - 1.5\cdot IQRQ1​−1.5⋅IQR or above Q3+1.5⋅IQRQ_3 + 1.5\cdot IQRQ3​+1.5⋅IQR.

13P¶

  • parameter — A numerical summary of a population (usually unknown); written with Greek letters, e.g. μ\muμ, ppp.

  • percentile — The value below which a stated percentage of the data falls.

  • placebo / blinding — A placebo is a fake treatment that looks real; blinding keeps subjects (and sometimes researchers) unaware of who received which treatment.

  • point estimate — A single statistic used as the best guess at a parameter (e.g., p^\hat{p}p^​ for ppp, xˉ\bar{x}xˉ for μ\muμ).

  • population — The entire group a study wants to learn about.

  • population proportion (ppp) — The true share of a population that has some trait; a parameter, usually unknown.

  • probability (P(A)P(A)P(A)) — A number from 0 (never) to 1 (always) measuring how likely event AAA is; interpreted here as a long-run relative frequency.

  • p-value — The probability of a test statistic at least this extreme, assuming H0H_0H0​ is true.

14Q¶

  • quartiles (Q1Q_1Q1​, Q3Q_3Q3​) — The 25th and 75th percentiles.

15R¶

  • random assignment — Randomly deciding which treatment each subject receives; distinct from random sampling.

  • random variable (XXX) — A variable that assigns a number to the outcome of a random process.

  • range — Maximum minus minimum.

  • relative frequency — A category’s count divided by the total nnn; a proportion or percent.

  • resistant (robust) statistic — A statistic that outliers and skew barely change (median, IQR); the mean and SD are not resistant.

16S¶

  • sample — The subset of a population actually observed.

  • sample mean (xˉ\bar{x}xˉ) — The average of a sample; the point estimate of μ\muμ.

  • sample proportion (p^\hat{p}p^​) — x/nx/nx/n, the share of a sample with a trait; the point estimate of ppp.

  • sample space (SSS) — The complete list of possible outcomes of a process.

  • sample standard deviation (sss) — s2\sqrt{s^2}s2​; the typical distance of a value from xˉ\bar{x}xˉ, in original units.

  • sample variance (s2s^2s2) — The average squared distance from the mean, ∑(xi−xˉ)2/(n−1)\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2/(n-1)∑(xi​−xˉ)2/(n−1).

  • sampling distribution — The distribution of a statistic’s value across every possible sample of a given size.

  • sampling variability — The natural tendency of a statistic to differ from sample to sample.

  • segmented (stacked) bar chart / mosaic plot — Graphs that show conditional proportions within each category, useful for spotting association.

  • ShadeNorm(lower, upper, μ, σ) — TI-83/84 draw command that shades the requested region under a Normal curve.

  • significance level (α\alphaα) — The pre-chosen risk of a wrong rejection; commonly 0.05.

  • simple random sample (SRS) — A sample drawn so that every possible sample of size nnn has an equal chance of selection.

  • skewed distribution — An asymmetric distribution with a longer tail on one side (right-skewed: mean > median).

  • standard deviation of a random variable (σ\sigmaσ, SD(X)SD(X)SD(X)) — ∑(x−μ)2P(x)\sqrt{\sum(x - \mu)^2 P(x)}∑(x−μ)2P(x)​; the typical distance an outcome falls from μ\muμ.

  • standard error (SE) — The standard deviation of a sampling distribution; SEp^=p^(1−p^)/nSE_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n}SEp^​​=p^​(1−p^​)/n​ and SExˉ=s/nSE_{\bar{x}} = s/\sqrt{n}SExˉ​=s/n​.

  • standard error of the mean (SExˉSE_{\bar{x}}SExˉ​) — s/ns/\sqrt{n}s/n​; how much sample means typically vary from the true population mean.

  • standard normal distribution (N(0,1)N(0,1)N(0,1)) — The Normal model with mean 0 and SD 1; what any Normal distribution becomes once standardized.

  • standardizing — Converting a value (or a whole distribution) to z-scores so it can be compared on a common scale.

  • statistic — A numerical summary of a sample, computed from data; written with Roman letters, e.g. xˉ\bar{x}xˉ, p^\hat{p}p^​.

  • statistically significant — The decision reached when the p-value is less than α\alphaα.

  • stratified sample — Random sampling done separately within predefined subgroups (strata).

  • success–failure condition — The check (np^≥10n\hat{p} \ge 10np^​≥10 and n(1−p^)≥10n(1-\hat{p}) \ge 10n(1−p^​)≥10, or np0≥10np_0 \ge 10np0​≥10 and n(1−p0)≥10n(1-p_0) \ge 10n(1−p0​)≥10 for a test) that justifies a Normal-based procedure for a proportion.

  • symmetric distribution — Roughly a mirror image on both sides of the center.

17T¶

  • t-distribution — A bell-shaped curve like the Normal but with heavier tails, used for one-mean inference when σ\sigmaσ is unknown; indexed by its degrees of freedom.

  • test statistic — A standardized measure (here zzz or ttt) of how far a sample statistic is from the value H0H_0H0​ claims, in standard-error units.

  • TInterval / T-Test — TI-83/84 menu items (under STAT ▸ TESTS) for a one-mean confidence interval and hypothesis test.

  • t.test() / tsum.test() / zsum.test() — R functions for one-mean inference: t.test() from raw data, tsum.test() from summary statistics, and zsum.test() as the large-sample z equivalent.

  • treatment / control group — The condition applied to a group; the control group gets no treatment (or a standard one) for comparison.

  • two-way (contingency) table — A table cross-tabulating two categorical variables.

  • Type I error — Rejecting a true H0H_0H0​; occurs with probability α\alphaα by design.

  • Type II error — Failing to reject a false H0H_0H0​.

18U¶

  • unbiased — Describes a statistic whose sampling distribution is centered on the true parameter (it does not systematically run high or low).

  • union (“or”) — The event that at least one of AAA or BBB happens.

  • unimodal / bimodal — A distribution with one peak, or with two peaks.

19V¶

  • variable — One column of a data table; a characteristic that can vary from case to case.

  • variance (σ2\sigma^2σ2, s2s^2s2) — The (probability-)weighted average of squared deviations; its square root is the standard deviation.

20W¶

  • weighted average — An average in which some values count more because they are multiplied by a weight (e.g., a probability) before summing.

  • which-test decision language — The habit of reading a scenario for its variable type (categorical / numerical) and its verb (estimate / test) before choosing a procedure.

21Z¶

  • z-score — z=(x−μ)/σz = (x - \mu)/\sigmaz=(x−μ)/σ; how many standard deviations a value is above (positive) or below (negative) the mean.

  • z-table — A printed table of cumulative areas P(Z<z)P(Z < z)P(Z<z) under the standard Normal curve, indexed by zzz to two decimals.
