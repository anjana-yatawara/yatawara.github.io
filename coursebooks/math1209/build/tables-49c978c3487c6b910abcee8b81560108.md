# Statistical Tables

This appendix collects the two tables MATH 1209 needs all semester: the **standard
Normal (z) table** (Ch 4–7) and the **Student t table** (Ch 7). Both answer the same
kind of question two different ways — "how much area?" or "what cutoff value?" — and
both have a calculator shortcut and an R shortcut, so you never have to read the
printed grid unless you want to check your work by hand. Every value below was
computed directly (not copied from another book), so it matches exactly what
`invNorm`, `normalcdf`, `qnorm`, and `qt` return on your own calculator or in R.

## 1. The standard Normal (z) table

The z-table gives the **area to the left** of a z-score under the standard Normal
curve $N(0,1)$ — that is, $P(Z < z)$. Find the whole-number-and-tenths part of your
z-score down the left column, then the hundredths part across the top row; the cell
where they meet is the area.

:::{tip} How to read it
Look up $z = 1.42$: find the row `1.4`, then the column `.02`. The cell reads
**0.9222**, meaning about 92.22% of the standard Normal curve lies below $z = 1.42$.
To find the area **above** a value, subtract from 1: $P(Z > 1.42) = 1 - 0.9222 =
0.0778$. To find the area **between** two z-scores, subtract the smaller table value
from the larger one.
:::

## 2. The Student t table

The **t-distribution** is used instead of the Normal whenever you estimate a mean's
standard error from the *sample* standard deviation $s$ rather than knowing the true
$\sigma$ — which is the everyday case in Ch 7. It is bell-shaped and centered at 0
like the Normal, but with heavier tails; its exact shape depends on the **degrees of
freedom**, $df = n - 1$ for one-sample work. Unlike the z-table, the t-table does not
print every possible area — it prints only the **critical values** ($t^{*}$) you need
for common confidence levels and significance levels, because that is what a
confidence interval or a hypothesis-test decision actually requires.

:::{tip} How to read it
Find your $df$ down the left column, then the column that matches your confidence
level or significance level, and read off $t^{*}$. For a **95% confidence interval**
with $n = 24$ (so $df = 23$), use the "0.025 one-tail / 95% CI" column: $t^{*} =
2.069$. For a **one-sided test at $\alpha = 0.05$** with $df = 23$, use the "0.05
one-tail" column: $t = 1.714$ is your cutoff. If your exact $df$ is not listed
(the table skips from 30 to 40), use the next **smaller** $df$ shown — it gives a
slightly wider, more conservative interval — or get the exact value with `invT` or
`qt()`.
:::

the matching z-critical value from the Course Formula Sheet (1.645 / 1.960 / 2.576 for
90% / 95% / 99%). That is the t-distribution's heavier tails fading away as the sample
gives you more information about $\sigma$ — with a huge sample, "not knowing $\sigma$"
barely costs you anything.

**Calculator + R equivalents.**

| You want… | TI-83/84 | R (`mosaic`) |
|---|---|---|
| Critical value $t^{*}$ (given confidence level, $df$) | `invT(area, df)` (TI-84; area is *to the left*) — or read `TInterval`'s output directly | `qt(p, df)` |
| p-value from a t statistic | `tcdf(lower, upper, df)` | `pt(t, df)` |
| Full interval or test (no table needed) | `STAT` ▸ `TESTS` ▸ `TInterval` / `T-Test` | `t.test(...)` / `tsum.test(...)` |

In practice, the `TInterval`, `T-Test`, `t.test()`, and `tsum.test()` procedures on
the [Course Formula Sheet](formulas.md) and the [TI-83/84 Quick Guide](ti-guide.md)
already look up $t^{*}$ for you — this table exists so you can see *where that number
came from* and check it by hand.
