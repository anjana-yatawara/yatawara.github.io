Before You Prompt: The 3 Questions

The BRIEFS framework (Module 4) gives you a structured way to design your first message. But if BRIEFS feels like too much, start with just three questions:

The 3 Questions (Simplified BRIEFS)
  1. What do I want? (Your intent — the single most important signal)
  2. Who am I? (Your background and expertise level)
  3. How will I use this? (What the output is for — the stakes)

That’s it. Answer those three before you type, and you’ll outperform 90% of AI users.

Example — The 3 Questions in action:

❌ Without: “Tell me about marketing.”

✅ With: “I’m a small bakery owner [who am I] and I want 5 ideas for increasing foot traffic during weekday afternoons [what do I want]. These need to be low-budget — under $200 each — because I’ll pick one to try this month [how will I use this].”

PART 1: WRITING AND COMMUNICATION

Emails

🟢 Quick Professional Email > Write a professional email to [recipient/role] about [topic]. Keep it under [100/150/200] words. Tone: [friendly but professional / formal / casual]. Include a clear ask with a specific deadline.

🟢 Email Reply > Draft a reply to this email. My situation: [brief context]. Tone: [grateful / firm / neutral / apologetic]. Keep it under [N] words. End with a clear next step. > > [Paste the email you’re replying to]

🟡 Difficult Conversation Email > I need to email [person/role] about [sensitive topic]. The relationship matters — I want to [maintain rapport / set a boundary / deliver bad news / ask for something uncomfortable]. Write a draft that: addresses the issue directly without being aggressive, acknowledges their perspective, proposes a specific path forward, and stays under 150 words.

🟡 Email Tone Adjustment > Rewrite this email in a [more formal / more casual / more diplomatic / more direct] tone. Preserve all factual content. Don’t add information I didn’t include. > > [Paste email]

🟢 Thank-You Email After Meeting > Write a brief thank-you email after meeting with [person/role] about [topic]. Mention one specific thing we discussed. Confirm the next step we agreed on. Under 80 words. Tone: genuine, not formulaic.

🟢 Scheduling Email > Write an email to [person] proposing a meeting about [topic]. Suggest 3 time slots. Mention it should take about [duration]. Include what we’ll cover so they can prepare. Under 100 words.

PART 2: RESEARCH AND ANALYSIS

🟢 Topic Overview > Give me a beginner-friendly overview of [topic]. Cover: what it is, why it matters, the 3 most important things to know, and one common misconception. Keep it under 300 words. No jargon — explain any technical terms you use.

🟢 Source Finder > I’m researching [topic] for [purpose]. Suggest 5 specific types of sources I should look for, and for each one, tell me WHERE to search (specific databases, websites, organizations — not just “Google it”). My audience is [who]. I need [academic / practical / news-based] sources.

🟡 Analyze This Data > I have this data: [paste data or describe it]. I need to understand: (1) what patterns or trends are present, (2) what’s surprising or unexpected, (3) what questions I should ask next. I’m [your role] and this is for [purpose]. Explain findings in plain language.

🟡 Literature Review Helper > I’m reviewing research on [topic] for [purpose]. I’ve found these studies: [list what you have]. Help me: (1) organize them by theme, (2) identify what they agree on, (3) spot gaps or contradictions, (4) suggest what kinds of studies I’m still missing. Don’t fabricate any citations.

🔴 Counterargument Generator > I’m arguing that [your position]. Steel-man the opposition. Give me the 3 strongest counterarguments a smart, well-informed critic would make. For each: state it clearly, explain why it’s compelling, and suggest how I should address it. Be genuinely challenging.

🟢 Summarize This for My Boss > Summarize this [article / report / document] in 3-5 bullet points for someone who has 2 minutes and no background in [field]. Each bullet: one key takeaway in plain language. End with: “The one thing to remember is…” > > [Paste content]

Include: the recommending body (AHA, CDC, WHO, etc.), key recommendations, and any recent changes. Flag anything where guidelines disagree. Don’t make up citations — tell me where to verify.

I’m a [teacher / administrator] evaluating whether to adopt [program / strategy / technology]. Help me build a research summary: what does the evidence say about effectiveness? What student populations has it been tested with? What are common implementation challenges? What would a fair pilot look like at my school?

I run a [type of business] and I’m considering [strategic decision]. Help me think through: market size and trends, 3 comparable businesses that have done this (tell me who to look up), the key risks, and what data I’d need to make a confident decision.

I manage [crop/livestock] in the [San Joaquin Valley / Central Valley]. I’m dealing with [specific issue — pest pressure / water allocation / soil salinity / labor costs]. What are the most recent research-backed approaches? Point me to UC Davis / UC ANR / USDA resources. Don’t give me generic advice — I need specifics for my climate zone and crop.

I’m a [role] at [agency/department] researching [policy topic]. I need: (1) what comparable jurisdictions have done, (2) what the evidence says about outcomes, (3) the main arguments for and against, (4) implementation considerations specific to [your region]. This will go in a briefing for [audience].

PART 3: DATA AND NUMBERS

🟢 Explain This Chart > Look at this [chart / graph / table] and explain what it shows in plain language. What’s the main takeaway? What trends are visible? Is anything surprising? Explain it to someone who doesn’t read charts often. > > [Upload or describe the chart]

🟡 Data Cleaning Helper > I have a spreadsheet with [describe the data]. The problems are: [missing values / inconsistent formatting / duplicates / messy columns / mixed data types]. Walk me through a step-by-step plan to clean it. I’m using [Excel / Google Sheets / R / Python]. Give me the specific formulas or code.

🟢 Spreadsheet Formula Helper > I have a spreadsheet in [Excel / Google Sheets] with [describe your data]. I need a formula that [what you want it to do]. The data is in columns [which columns] and I want the result in [where]. Give me the formula and explain what each part does.

🟡 Survey Design > I need a survey about [topic] for [audience]. Purpose: [what decisions will this data inform]. Design: [N] questions, mix of [rating scales / multiple choice / open-ended]. Keep it under [time] minutes. Avoid: leading questions, double-barreled questions, and jargon.

🔴 Statistical Test Chooser > I have [describe your data — sample size, variables, what you’re comparing]. My research question is: [state it]. What statistical test should I use? Explain why, what assumptions to check, and how to interpret the result. I’m using [R / SPSS / Excel / Python]. Give me the code or steps.

  • Sales/Revenue
  • Public Health Data
  • Academic Grades

I’m reviewing my [department / business / household] budget for [time period]. Here’s the data: [paste or describe]. Help me identify: the top 3 spending categories, any trends, items that seem unusual, and where I have room to cut or reallocate.

Here’s my [monthly / quarterly] sales data: [paste]. I need to understand: which products/services drive the most revenue, whether there’s seasonality, which customers/segments are growing vs declining, and a realistic forecast for next [period].

I have [county / state] health data on [topic — vaccination rates / chronic disease / ER visits]. Help me: identify disparities across [demographics / zip codes / communities], spot trends over [time period], compare to state/national benchmarks, and suggest what to investigate next.

Here are my students’ scores on [assessment]: [paste data]. Help me understand: the distribution, which questions students struggled with most, whether there are performance gaps across [sections / demographics], and what I should reteach.

🟢 Meeting Agenda Builder > Build a [30/45/60]-minute meeting agenda for [topic]. Attendees: [who]. Goals: [what we need to decide]. Include: time allocations, a clear owner for each item, one decision to make, and 5 minutes at the end for action items.

🟢 To-Do List Prioritizer > Here’s everything on my plate: [dump your full list]. I have about [N] hours of work time today.

🟡 Weekly Planning Session > It’s [day]. Here’s what I need to accomplish this week: [list everything]. My constraints: [meetings, deadlines, commitments]. Help me build a realistic day-by-day plan. Flag anything that won’t fit.

🟡 Process Documentation > I need to document the process for [task that you do regularly]. Write step-by-step instructions that a new person could follow without asking questions. Include: prerequisites, each step in order, common mistakes, and who to ask if something goes wrong.

🟢 Decision Matrix > I’m choosing between [Option A], [Option B], and [Option C] for [decision]. The factors that matter most: [list 4-6 criteria]. Score each on each factor (1-5), weight by importance, and give me a recommendation with reasoning.

I run [business type] with [N] employees. This week: [dump everything]. Revenue goal: [X]. Help me build a weekly plan that balances customer-facing work, back-office tasks, and the one strategic thing I keep putting off: [what is it].

PART 6: TEACHING AND LEARNING

🟢 Explain Like I’m… > Explain [concept] to [audience — a 10-year-old / a freshman / a CEO / my grandmother]. Use one analogy, no jargon, and check my understanding with 2 questions at the end.

🟡 Assessment Builder > Create a [quiz / exam / worksheet] on [topic] for [audience level]. Include: [N] multiple choice, [N] short answer, [N] application problems. For each: correct answer and explanation. Align to: [learning objectives].

🟡 The SMARTS Tutor (from Module 4) > Adopt the persona of a supportive tutor. Restate questions to confirm understanding. Ask what I already know. Offer hints before answers. If I ask for the full solution, encourage step-by-step reasoning first. Keep responses brief. Summarize what I learned when we finish. Wait for my question.

🟡 Lesson Plan Builder > Design a [30/45/60/90]-minute lesson on [topic] for [grade level / audience]. Include: learning objectives, hook (5 min), direct instruction (15 min), guided practice (15 min), independent practice (10 min), closing (5 min). One differentiation strategy and one extension.

🟢 Study Guide Generator > I have an exam on [subject] covering [topics]. Create a study guide: key terms with definitions, the 5 most important concepts, 3 practice problems with solutions, and common student mistakes.

🔴 Rubric Generator > Create an assessment rubric for [assignment type]. 4-5 dimensions. For each: performance at 4 levels (Excellent, Proficient, Developing, Not Yet). Use specific, observable criteria — not vague language.

🟡 Discussion Question Builder > I’m teaching [topic] to [audience]. Create 5 discussion questions from recall to analysis to application. For each: the question, cognitive level, and one follow-up probe for surface-level answers.

🟢 Parent Conference Prep > Meeting with parents of a student who [situation]. Prepare: 3 data points to share, 2 positive observations to lead with, the main concern framed constructively, 2 actionable next steps, and 2 questions to ask the parents.

  • K-5
  • Middle/High School
  • College
  • Adult/Professional

Create a [15/20/30]-minute activity for [grade] learning [topic]. Must include: manipulatives or movement, a visual component, and a formative check. I have 25 students and one adult.

Design a project-based learning activity on [topic] for [grade]. Takes [N] class periods. Must connect to their lives. Include: driving question, deliverable, rubric criteria, and one tech integration.

Design a flipped classroom session on [topic] for [audience]. Include: what students do BEFORE class (20 min), IN class (50 min — active learning, not lecture), and AFTER (15 min).

Design a [30/60/90]-minute training on [topic] for [audience]. Don’t make it feel like school. Include: relevant opening hook, one concept delivered through activity (not slides), practice round, and a clear takeaway for Monday morning.

PART 11: SMALL BUSINESS AND ENTREPRENEURSHIP

🟢 Business Plan Quick Draft > Starting a business in [location]. Target customers: [who]. Investment: [amount]. Write a lean plan: the opportunity (3 sentences), target customer, marketing approach, startup costs, revenue model. Under 2 pages. For [my planning / a loan / a pitch].

🟡 Pricing Strategy > I sell [product/service] to [customer type]. Current price: [amount]. Competitor prices: [if known]. My costs: [fixed and variable]. Am I priced right? Build 3 scenarios (budget / market / premium) with projected revenue. I’m in [location] — not San Francisco.

🟢 Customer Review Response > Respond to this review. Star rating: [N]. Complaint: [summarize]. I want to: [apologize / explain / resolve / clarify]. Professional, empathetic, human — not corporate-scripted. Under 100 words. For [Yelp / Google / Facebook].

🟡 Competitive Analysis > I run [business type] in [location]. Top 3 competitors: [name/describe them]. Analyze: what they do well, weaknesses, pricing, marketing, and my differentiation opportunity. Comparison table plus “your edge” summary.

🟡 Cash Flow Forecast > Build a 6-month cash flow projection. Monthly revenue: [amount]. Fixed costs: [list]. Variable costs: [list]. Upcoming expenses: [list]. Show: when I’ll be tight, cash reserve needed, and whether I can afford [planned expenditure] in month [N].

🟡 Hiring First Employee > I run business solo and I’m hiring my first employee for [role]. I’m in [state]. Walk me through: (1) legal prerequisites (EIN, workers comp, etc.), (2) where to post, (3) job description template, (4) offer letter basics, (5) onboarding. I’ve never done this.

PART 12: PERSONAL AND LIFE MANAGEMENT

🟢 Financial Decision Helper > Deciding whether to [financial decision]. My situation: [income, debt, savings, dependents, goals]. Walk me through the key factors. Don’t tell me what to do — help me think systematically. What info do I need before deciding?

🟢 Letter or Speech Writer > Write a [toast / eulogy / thank-you / recommendation / letter] for [occasion]. About: [person, relationship]. Key stories/qualities: [2-3]. Tone: [heartfelt / funny / formal / casual]. Length: [N] minutes / [N] words.

🟢 Difficult Conversation Script > I need to talk to [person/relationship] about [topic]. I want to: [set a boundary / ask for something / address a problem]. Help me plan: how to open, what to say, what NOT to say, how to respond if they get [defensive / emotional / dismissive]. Keep it real — not therapy-speak.

🟡 Travel Planning > Planning a [length] trip to [destination] in [month]. Budget: [amount]. Travelers: [who]. Priorities: [food / culture / adventure / relaxation / budget]. Build a day-by-day itinerary with activities, costs, logistics, and one thing tourists miss.

🟢 Complaint Letter > Formal complaint to [company/agency] about [issue]. What happened: [describe]. What I’ve tried: [list]. What I want: [resolution]. Firm and factual, not angry. Include dates, reference numbers, deadline for response. Under 250 words.

PART 13: TRADES, CONSTRUCTION, AND MAINTENANCE

🟢 Estimate Builder > Build a customer estimate for [job type]. Scope: [describe]. Materials: [list what you know]. Labor: [rate / crew / hours]. Professional format with line items, material costs (I’ll fill in prices), labor, total. Notes section for warranty and payment terms.

🟡 Troubleshooting Guide > Diagnosing [problem] in [system]. Symptoms: [describe]. Checked so far: [list]. Walk me through systematic diagnosis — most likely to least likely. For each: what to check, what tool I need, what the fix is.

🟢 Safety Toolbox Talk > 10-minute safety talk for my crew on [topic]. Practical, not generic OSHA slides. Include: one real incident example, the 3 most important rules, one thing to check before starting today, and a question for the crew. Conversational — these are journeymen.

🟡 Code Compliance Checklist > Doing a [project type] in [jurisdiction]. Build a checklist of [building / electrical / plumbing] code requirements. [Residential / commercial / existing / new construction]. Include: permit requirements, inspection points, common violations.

🟢 Customer Communication > Draft a text/email to a customer about [situation — delay / additional damage / scope change / job complete]. Honest about [the issue]. Include: what happened, impact (cost/time), recommendation, next steps. Under 100 words. Build trust, not upsell.

PART 14: CUSTOM GPT AND AI TOOL DESIGN

Note: “Custom GPTs” refers to OpenAI’s implementation. Other providers offer similar features under different names (Claude Projects, Google Gems, etc.). The system prompt patterns below work across platforms.

🟡 The Universal Tutor > You are a [subject] tutor for [level]. Knowledge base: uploaded files. Rules: restate questions, ask what they know, hints before answers, encourage step-by-step reasoning, summarize what was learned, stay within uploaded materials, say so if you can’t answer.

🟡 The FAQ Bot > You are an assistant for [org/dept]. Answer using ONLY uploaded documents. Concise (2-3 sentences). Reference which document. If not in documents: “I don’t have that. Contact [person/email].” Never fabricate. Tone: [friendly / formal].

🔴 The Workflow Assistant > You are a [role] assistant for [task type]. When given a task: (1) confirm and ask 1-2 clarifying questions, (2) propose steps, (3) execute showing work, (4) confirm before proceeding, (5) summarize at the end. Never: [prohibited actions].

PART 15: VERIFICATION AND CRITICAL THINKING

🟢 Fact-Check This Output > Fact-check this AI output: identify suspicious claims, unverifiable statistics, possibly fabricated citations, and logical gaps. For each: tell me what to search to verify. > > [Paste AI output]

🟡 Challenge My Thinking > I believe [your position]. Present the 3 strongest counterarguments. For each: state the argument, explain why it’s compelling, and what evidence would change your mind. Be genuinely challenging.

🟡 Identify What’s Missing > Review this [plan / proposal / analysis]. What am I NOT considering? Risks? Missing audiences? What data would strengthen or weaken my conclusions? Perspective of a skeptical but fair reviewer.

🟢 Red Team My Plan > Here’s my plan for [what]. Try to make it fail. What goes wrong? What did I underestimate? What blindsides me? 5 failure scenarios ranked by likelihood, with one mitigation each.

🟡 AI Disclosure Statement > Write a disclosure about how I used AI in [context]. AI was used for: [list]. Human contribution: [list]. Audience: [who]. Under 100 words. Honest and specific.

PART 16: CAPSTONE PROJECT PROMPTS

🟡 Capstone Scoping > I’m planning my AI literacy capstone project. My field is [field] and my role is [role/student status]. I’m choosing Option [A/B/C/D]. Help me narrow my scope: I need a project that can be completed in 10-15 hours, tested with real users, and produces a tangible deliverable. My initial idea is [describe]. Is this too broad? Help me narrow it to one specific, testable problem.

🟡 Integration Plan Helper > I want to bring AI into [specific workflow] at [organization type]. Currently this process takes [time] and involves [describe steps]. Help me design an AI-augmented version: which steps could AI handle, what tools would I use, what’s the human review checkpoint, and what could go wrong? Frame this as a proposal I could present to my manager.

🟡 Custom GPT Design > I’m building a custom GPT for [purpose] targeting [audience]. The knowledge base will include [list documents]. Help me write system instructions that: (1) define the GPT’s role clearly, (2) set boundaries on what it won’t do, (3) include the ELEVATE 7-step tutoring algorithm if appropriate, and (4) handle edge cases. Give me 5 test prompts to evaluate it.

🟡 Workshop Planning > I’m designing a [duration] AI workshop for [audience — who they are, how many, their tech comfort level]. The goal is [what participants should be able to do after]. Help me build: an agenda with time allocations, 3 key messages, one hands-on activity, and a pre/post survey question. The biggest risk is [what could go wrong].

🟡 Capstone Reflection > I just completed my capstone project: [brief description]. Help me write an honest reflection addressing: (1) what I planned vs what actually happened, (2) the most surprising finding, (3) what failed and what I learned from the failure, (4) how I applied BRIEFS and SMARTS, (5) what I would do differently, and (6) how this project changed my thinking about AI. Push me to be specific — no generic “I learned a lot” allowed.

❌ Prompt Anti-Patterns: Don’t Do This

These are the most common prompting mistakes. If you’re getting bad results, you’re probably doing one of these:

Anti-Pattern Why It Fails Fix
“Tell me about X” No intent, no context, no constraints. AI gives a Wikipedia summary. Add who you are, what you need it for, and how you’ll use it (The 3 Questions).
“Write me an essay” No audience, no length, no purpose. Gets generic 5-paragraph template. Specify: topic, audience, purpose, length, tone, and what it’s for.
Pasting 2,000 words then asking “What do you think?” The question is too vague. AI doesn’t know what dimension to evaluate. Ask specific questions: “Is the argument in paragraph 3 convincing? Why or why not?”
“Make it better” Better how? More concise? More formal? More persuasive? AI guesses. Say exactly what “better” means: “Make it shorter, more direct, and cut the jargon.”
Asking for the answer instead of help thinking You get the answer but don’t learn. On the exam, you’re stuck. Use SMARTS: “Give me a hint, not the answer. What concept should I apply here?”
Trusting the first response First drafts are drafts. AI defaults to safe, generic output. Always iterate: “Good start. Now make it [specific adjustment].”
“Act as an expert in everything” Jack of all trades = master of none. Responses stay surface-level. Give a specific role: “You are a pediatric nurse educator” not “you are an expert.”
Entering sensitive data into free-tier tools Student records, patient data, financial info — potentially exposed. Check your tool’s data policy. Use enterprise tiers or local models for sensitive work.
