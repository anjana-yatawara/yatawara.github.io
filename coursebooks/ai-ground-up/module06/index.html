AI for Everyday Productivity

Module 6 · Write, Organize, Create, Automate

Content current as of April 2026. AI tools evolve rapidly — underlying principles remain applicable.

Learning Objectives

Builds on: Modules 4-5 (prompting + research skills)

After completing this module, you will be able to:

  1. Use AI to draft, edit, and refine professional writing in minutes
  2. Apply the “Critic, not Ghostwriter” approach to improve your own work with AI
  3. Build a personal prompt library for tasks you do repeatedly
  4. Use AI for organization: meeting notes, project plans, schedules, budgets
  5. Use AI for creative work: brand design, presentations, social media content
  6. Build simple tools and apps with natural language (“vibe coding”)
  7. Evaluate AI tools before committing — the 5-question test

Two Tuesdays

Tuesday without AI:

Sarah, a program coordinator at a community nonprofit, arrives at 8:30 AM. She spends 45 minutes drafting a grant progress report, rewriting the executive summary three times. She spends 30 minutes composing emails to four partner organizations, each needing a slightly different tone. After lunch, she takes an hour to create slides for Thursday’s board meeting, struggling with formatting. She spends 40 minutes manually organizing meeting notes from last week into action items. At 4 PM she realizes she still hasn’t started the donor thank-you letters. She stays until 6:15.

Tuesday with AI:

Sarah arrives at 8:30 AM. She pastes her quarterly data into Claude and uses a BRIEFS-structured prompt to generate a draft progress report in 4 minutes. She edits for accuracy and voice — done in 20 minutes total. She drafts all four emails using a prompt template she built last month, adjusting each in 2 minutes — 15 minutes total. After lunch, she uploads her notes to ChatGPT and asks for a slide outline with speaker notes — she refines it into a polished deck in 30 minutes. She pastes last week’s meeting transcript into NotebookLM and extracts action items in 3 minutes. At 3 PM she generates personalized donor thank-you letters using a template prompt that pulls from her donor database. She leaves at 4:45 with everything done, including 30 minutes she spent learning a new AI tool she’d been curious about.

Same job. Same person. Same competence. The difference isn’t talent or work ethic — it’s workflow design. Sarah didn’t become smarter. She became AI-augmented.

Without AI: A clinic manager spends 45 minutes writing follow-up care instructions for 8 discharged patients, each with different conditions. She spends 30 minutes on insurance pre-authorization paperwork. She manually reviews patient satisfaction surveys for monthly report.

With AI: She uses a BRIEFS-structured template to generate personalized care instructions in 2 minutes each (16 min total, then reviews for accuracy). Pre-auth paperwork is drafted by AI from structured inputs (10 min). Patient surveys are analyzed by AI with themes extracted (5 min). She leaves an hour early — and spends 20 minutes of that reading about a new clinical AI tool.

Without AI: A teacher spends 90 minutes creating a quiz for Chapter 7, writing 20 questions from scratch. She spends 40 minutes writing report card comments for 28 students. She spends 30 minutes formatting a lesson plan for an observation.

With AI: She prompts Claude with Chapter 7 learning objectives and gets 30 draft questions in 5 minutes, selects and refines 20 (25 min total). Report card comments are drafted from her grade book notes (15 min to generate + review). Lesson plan is reformatted with AI to match the observation template (10 min). Saved: 110 minutes.

Without AI: A bakery owner spends an hour writing this week’s social media posts (7 posts). She spends 45 minutes updating her website menu descriptions. She spends 30 minutes responding to customer review replies.

With AI: Social media posts drafted with a prompt template she built once (15 min to generate + customize). Menu descriptions updated from a master ingredients list (10 min). Review responses generated with appropriate tone for each (15 min). Saved: 95 minutes. She uses them to experiment with a new recipe.

Without AI: A county program coordinator spends 2 hours compiling data from 4 departments into a board report. She drafts 6 email summaries to different stakeholders. She writes a policy FAQ update.

With AI: Board report compiled using AI to merge and summarize data sources (45 min with verification). Email summaries generated from the same report, tone-adjusted per stakeholder (20 min). FAQ update drafted by AI referencing the latest policy documents (15 min). Saved: 100 minutes.

Sarah is fictional. The underlying numbers are real.

In 2023, researchers from Harvard Business School, Wharton, MIT, and Warwick partnered with Boston Consulting Group to run a preregistered randomized controlled trial on AI’s effect on knowledge work. They recruited 758 BCG consultants and randomly assigned them to complete 18 realistic consulting tasks either with or without GPT-4 access (Dell’Acqua et al., 2023; peer-reviewed version, Dell’Acqua et al., 2025, Organization Science).

For tasks inside the capability frontier of the AI — drafting, ideating, structuring arguments, analyzing interview notes — the AI-assisted group completed 12.2% more tasks, completed them 25.1% faster, and produced work rated 40% higher in quality by independent graders. Consultants whose baseline performance was below the group average improved 43% — the authors called this the “performance equalizer” effect.

But the study also included one task that was designed to fall outside the AI’s capability frontier. On that task, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce the correct answer. The AI confidently produced plausible but wrong output, and consultants who deferred to it got worse results than they would have on their own.

The authors coined the term “jagged technological frontier” — AI capabilities are uneven and the edges aren’t obvious. Two tasks that look identical in difficulty can sit on opposite sides of what the AI actually does well.

Sarah’s Tuesday works because her tasks sit inside the frontier. The rest of this module teaches you how to tell the difference.

Common Misconceptions About AI Productivity
  • “AI saves time automatically” — It saves time when you INVEST time designing the right workflow. A bad prompt wastes more time than no prompt.
  • “AI writes better than me” — AI writes FASTER than you. Whether it writes better depends on the prompt, the task, and how much you edit. The Critic, not Ghostwriter approach preserves YOUR quality.
  • “Prompt libraries are cheating” — They’re efficiency. A surgeon doesn’t reinvent the checklist for each operation. You don’t reinvent the prompt for each email.
  • “Vibe coding = real software engineering” — It’s great for prototypes and personal tools. For anything involving security, reliability, or scale, you need a real developer.
  • “If I use AI for everything, I’ll forget how to do things myself” — This is the cognitive offloading trap from Module 1. Use AI to handle mechanical tasks. Keep the judgment and creativity muscles exercised.

This module gives you Sarah’s toolkit.

Before We Start: How to Evaluate an AI Tool

New AI tools launch every week. Most aren’t worth your time. Before committing to any tool, run it through the 5-Question Test:

The 5-Question Test for AI Tools
  1. Does it solve a problem I actually have? (Not a cool demo — a real friction point in my workflow)
  2. Is it better than what I’m already doing? (Including the time to learn it)
  3. Where does my data go? (Cloud? Local? Shared with third parties? Used for training?)
  4. What does it cost — really? (Free tier limitations? Subscription creep? Hidden usage fees?)
  5. Will it still exist in 6 months? (Is the company funded? Does it have real users? Or is it a demo?)

If any answer makes you uneasy, keep looking. The best AI tool is the one you’ll actually use consistently — not the one with the most features.

AI for Writing and Communication

Writing is the #1 use case for generative AI. But there’s a right way and a wrong way.

The Wrong Way: AI as Ghostwriter

“Write a report about our Q3 fundraising results.”

The AI produces a generic report with placeholder language. You spend 30 minutes editing it to sound like you, fixing inaccuracies, adding actual numbers. You would have been faster writing from scratch.

The Right Way: AI as Critic, Coach, and Collaborator

The Critic Approach — write YOUR draft first, then use AI to improve it:

“You are my critical peer reviewer. Do not rewrite my text. Diagnose and explain.

Evaluate my draft using this rubric: 1. Purpose and audience fit 2. Executive summary clarity (under 120 words?) 3. Argument structure and flow 4. Evidence quality and citation needs 5. Actionable recommendations 6. Professional tone and formatting

For each item, give: a 2-3 sentence diagnosis, one high-impact fix, and one example sentence starter I can use.

At the end, list 3 sources or data points I should locate to strengthen the argument. Do not fabricate citations.”

This prompt — adapted from a real university classroom exercise — keeps YOUR voice in the writing while using AI to identify weaknesses you’d miss. The AI reviews; you revise. The output is genuinely yours, genuinely improved.

The Coach Approach — AI helps you improve the PROCESS of writing:

“I need to write a professional email to a professor requesting a deadline extension. I tend to over-explain and sound apologetic. Coach me: what should I include, what should I leave out, and what tone should I aim for? Don’t write the email — help me write it better.”

Ready-to-Use Writing Prompts

Professional Email Coach: > “Rewrite this draft email for clarity and tone. Keep it under 120 words. Include 2 subject line options and a polite ask with a specific time window. [Paste email]”

Executive Summary Check: > “Evaluate my executive summary. Is it a standalone 120-word overview with purpose, scope, key findings, and recommendations? Give a pass/fail and a 5-bullet rewrite plan.”

Tone Adjustment: > “Rewrite this paragraph for [audience]. Current tone: [casual/formal/technical]. Desired tone: [casual/formal/technical]. Preserve all factual content.”

Guided Practice 6.1

Take something you’ve written recently — an email, a report section, a proposal paragraph. Paste it into an AI chatbot with the Critic prompt above. Compare the AI’s feedback to your own assessment. Did it catch something you missed? Did it miss something you knew was weak?

What most people discover AI is excellent at catching structural issues (missing transitions, unclear topic sentences, inconsistent tone) and formatting problems. It’s weaker at judging whether your argument is convincing to your specific audience — that requires context the AI doesn’t have unless you provide it (BRIEFS!). The best workflow: AI catches the structural stuff so you can focus on the persuasive and strategic dimensions.
✅ Test Yourself: Writing with AI

Which approach produces better results — and why?

  1. “Write a report about our Q3 results.”
  2. Writing your own draft, then asking AI: “Review my draft using this rubric: [6 criteria]. Diagnose, don’t rewrite.”

(Answer: B — the Critic approach. You keep your voice, your knowledge, and your ownership. AI catches structural issues you’d miss. The output is genuinely yours, genuinely improved.)

AI for Organization and Planning

Meeting Notes → Action Items (in 60 seconds)

If your meetings generate notes or transcripts, AI can extract structure instantly:

“Here are notes from our team meeting. Extract: (1) decisions made, (2) action items with owner and deadline, (3) open questions that need follow-up, (4) topics deferred to next meeting. Format as a table.”

Project Planning

“I’m planning [event/project]. Timeline: [X weeks]. Team size: [N people]. Budget: [amount]. Generate a week-by-week project plan with milestones, task assignments, and a risk register with 5 likely risks and mitigation strategies.”

Study Planning (from BA 1028)

“I’m a first-year business major. I need to learn [topic], and my exam is in 10 days. Coach me with spaced practice. Build a 7-session plan with daily micro-tasks under 25 minutes, retrieval practice questions, and 3 mini-quizzes. At each step, ask me to produce an answer before revealing hints. Use a Socratic style.”

Budget Planning (from BA 1028)

“I have take-home income of $X per month and fixed costs of [list]. Build a student budget using a 50-30-20 baseline, a debt snowball if needed, and a 3-month emergency fund plan. Output a simple monthly table and a weekly checklist of actions. Ask me 5 follow-ups to personalize it.”

AI for Creative Work

The Brand Sprint: From Idea to Identity in 30 Minutes

This workflow, tested with university business students, takes a raw business idea and turns it into a brand brief with positioning, visual identity, and logo concepts — all AI-assisted.

The prompt (from BA 1028):

“Act as a brand strategist. Interview me with 8 questions to define the brand: target customer, value proposition, tone, 3 brand adjectives, competitor set, promise, and constraints (colors or symbols to avoid). Then propose: - A one-sentence positioning statement - A tagline with 3 variants - A mini brand kit: 3-color palette with hex codes, a heading font + body font, and a simple logo usage note - 3 logo concept directions written as prompts I can use in an image generation tool”

What this produced for students: A team creating a lemonade stand on campus got: positioning statement (“For students on the go, Roadrunner Lemonade serves fresh-squeezed, real-fruit lemonade that delivers a bright pick-me-up between classes”), three taglines (“Fresh squeeze, bright day” / “Sip sunny” / “Brighten your break”), a color palette (Sunshine Yellow #F6C700, Citrus Orange #FF8A00, Cool Blue #1F7A99), font pairing (Montserrat SemiBold + Lato Regular), and three image-generation prompts for different logo concepts.

30 minutes. From “we want a lemonade stand” to a complete brand identity. No design experience required.

Presentation Design

“I need to present [topic] to [audience] in [N] minutes. Create an outline with: slide titles, 2-3 bullet points per slide, speaker notes for each slide, and suggestions for one visual per slide. Aim for [N] slides maximum.”

Social Media Content

“I run a [type of business] targeting [audience]. Generate 5 social media post ideas for [platform]. Each should include: a hook (first line), body text under 100 words, 3 relevant hashtags, and a call to action. Tone: [professional/playful/informative].”

Brainstorming (from BA 1028)

“Act as a structured idea generator. Step 1: Ask me what context I’m brainstorming for (business, class project, event, or personal). Step 2: Generate 10 creative ideas across a variety of directions. Step 3: Group those ideas into 3 clusters or themes. Step 4: For each cluster, expand one idea into a 3-step action plan I could start on tomorrow. Always remind me that these are starting points and I should adapt or combine them.”

Vibe Coding: Building Tools Without Programming

One of the most surprising AI capabilities: you can build functional apps, dashboards, and tools by describing what you want in plain English. No programming experience required.

What You Can Build

  • A simple calculator or converter for your specific domain
  • An interactive quiz or assessment for your students/team
  • A budget tracker or project dashboard
  • A data visualization from a spreadsheet
  • A simple website or landing page
  • A form that collects and organizes responses

How It Works

Tools like Claude Artifacts, ChatGPT Canvas, Replit Agent, and v0 by Vercel let you describe what you want and the AI generates working code. You iterate by describing changes in natural language:

“Build me a simple web tool where I can paste a paragraph of text and it shows me: word count, sentence count, average sentence length, and reading level. Make it look clean with a blue and white color scheme.”

The AI produces a working tool. You test it. You say:

“Add a button that copies the results to my clipboard. Also add a section that suggests how to simplify sentences that are over 25 words.”

The AI updates the tool. No code written by you — just natural language descriptions of what you want.

Vibe Coding Limits

AI-generated code works great for simple, personal tools. It gets unreliable for complex applications, anything involving security (login systems, payment processing), or anything that needs to work perfectly every time (medical, legal, financial). Build toys and tools, not products. For anything serious, involve a real developer.

Building Your Prompt Library

The highest-leverage productivity habit isn’t learning new AI tools — it’s saving the prompts that work.

How to Build Your Library

  1. Start with tasks you do weekly. Email drafts, meeting notes, report sections, data formatting.
  2. Write and test prompts for each task. Use BRIEFS to structure them. Iterate until the output consistently meets your needs.
  3. Save them somewhere accessible. A Google Doc, a Notion page, a text file on your desktop, a Notes app folder.
  4. Include the template AND an example. Future-you will forget what each prompt does without an example output.
  5. Update quarterly. AI tools change. Prompts that worked 6 months ago may need refinement.

Prompt Library Starter Kit

Task Prompt Template
Weekly report “Summarize the following accomplishments, challenges, and next steps into a [X]-word weekly update for [audience]. Tone: [professional/casual]. Format: bullet points under 3 headers.”
Email reply “Draft a reply to this email. Context: [your situation]. Tone: [friendly/firm/neutral]. Length: under [N] words. Include: [specific ask or next step].”
Meeting prep “Given this agenda and these background documents, prepare: 3 questions I should ask, 2 points I should raise, and 1 potential concern to address.”
Data summary “Analyze this data and provide: key trends, notable outliers, and 3 actionable takeaways for [audience]. Include one recommended visualization.”
Content draft “Write a [type] about [topic] for [audience]. Tone: [X]. Length: [N] words. Include: [key points]. Avoid: [what not to include].”

Module 6 Summary

Key Takeaways

  1. AI doesn’t save time automatically. It saves time when you invest time designing the right workflow. The 30 minutes you spend crafting a prompt library saves hours over a semester.

  2. Critic, not Ghostwriter. Write your draft first, then use AI to review it. This keeps your voice, your ideas, and your ownership — while catching structural issues you’d miss.

  3. Ready-to-use prompts exist for email coaching, executive summary checks, tone adjustment, meeting notes extraction, project planning, study planning, budgeting, brand sprints, presentations, and brainstorming.

  4. The Brand Sprint workflow turns a raw business idea into a brand identity (positioning, tagline, colors, fonts, logo concepts) in 30 minutes. Tested with real university students.

  5. Vibe coding lets you build simple tools by describing what you want in plain English. Great for personal tools, dangerous for anything that needs to be secure or reliable.

  6. Build a prompt library. Save the prompts that work. Include templates AND examples. Update quarterly.

  7. The 5-Question Test before adopting any new AI tool: Does it solve a real problem? Is it better than my current approach? Where does my data go? What does it really cost? Will it still exist in 6 months?

💭 Think About It

The productivity gains from AI are real — but they create a new question: what do you do with the time you save? Sarah left work at 4:45 instead of 6:15. She could use that 90 minutes to do more work (productivity trap), scroll social media (wasted potential), or learn something new, mentor a colleague, or think strategically about her organization’s future (growth). AI is a tool that gives you back time. What you do with that time is the real decision. The most AI-augmented professionals don’t just work faster — they think deeper because they’ve freed up the space to do so.

🔬 Lab 6: The Workflow Transformation

Lab 6: The Workflow Transformation

Time: 60 minutes | Tools needed: Any AI chatbot + your actual work materials

Purpose: Transform one real workflow from your actual job, studies, or personal life.

Part A — Map Your Current Workflow (10 min) 1. Pick one task you do at least weekly that takes 30+ minutes. 2. Write out every step you currently do, in order. 3. Estimate the time each step takes. 4. Identify which steps are mechanical (formatting, organizing, drafting) vs intellectual (analyzing, deciding, judging).

Part B — Design the AI-Augmented Version (15 min) 5. For each mechanical step, write a prompt that could handle it (use BRIEFS). 6. For each intellectual step, write a prompt that could SUPPORT it (not replace it). 7. Identify which steps should remain fully human (judgment calls, relationship management, ethical decisions).

Part C — Test It Live (25 min) 8. Do the task using your AI-augmented workflow. Time each step. 9. Compare the output quality to what you’d normally produce.

Part D — Reflection (10 min) 10. Fill in this table and write 3-4 sentences reflecting on the experience:
